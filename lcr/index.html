LCR Meter

< Back

The entry price for a capable LCR meter is around $100 NZD even on AliExpress, so I set out to make a cheaper LCR meter myself. I was also intrigued by the challenge of designing an LCR meter and have used this project to expand my knowledge of analogue electronics. This project is currently in progress; I’ll update this page again once it’s completed.

Initially I had no idea how to go about designing an LCR, but eventually I came across a Texas Instruments design guide (TIDA-060029) and used it as the basis for my own design. The figure below shows the LCR circuit stripped down to its most fundamental components and shows the equation that is used to calculate the device under test (DUT) impedance. If the phase relationship of the output voltages is known, then the phase of the DUT impedance is known. The LCR software is able to convert the complex impedance into an inductance or capacitance value + ESR.


To allow measurement of a vast range of inductances and capacitances, the LCR was supposed to have 3 different range resistors. The first PCB revision used MOSFET bidirectional power switches to allow switching of range resistors. However, the MOSFETs had significant stray capacitance, introducing unwanted low pass filters into the signal path. To overcome this issue I plan to use signal relays for range resistor switching on subsequent PCB revisions. As a temporary workaround, I soldered jumpers to manually switched range resistors.

The first prototype PCB also had issues with switching noise being present in the LCR test waveform. I’m confident the noise is from the power supplies themselves, not from return current issues, as the analogue and digital sections should be separated sufficiently. In the next PCB revision I’ll add LC filtering to the switching power supplies to alleviate the noise problem.
